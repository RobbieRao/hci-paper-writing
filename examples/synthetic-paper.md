# Abstract

Community archivists often need to judge whether AI-generated descriptions are
grounded in source material. We present TraceLens, a prototype that exposes
provenance warnings during description review. Through interviews and structured
tasks with 12 archivists, we identify three breakdowns in how warnings communicate
uncertainty. We contribute design requirements for provenance-aware review tools.

# Introduction

Digital archives increasingly incorporate generated descriptions, but archivists
remain responsible for accuracy and accountability. Existing interfaces surface
model output without showing how individual phrases relate to evidence.

RQ1: How do community archivists interpret provenance warnings during review?

We introduce TraceLens and use it as a research artifact in a qualitative study.
We contribute: (1) an empirical account of three interpretation breakdowns and
(2) design requirements for provenance-aware archival workflows.

# Method

We recruited 12 community archivists through professional mailing lists. Each
participant completed two review tasks and a semi-structured interview. We
obtained informed consent and used reflexive thematic analysis. This synthetic
example does not represent a real study or ethics approval.

# Findings

Participants reported that source-level links were useful, but several interpreted
warning color as a correctness score. The themes indicate that provenance and
confidence must be represented separately.

# Discussion

The findings suggest that archival AI interfaces should preserve the distinction
between evidentiary traceability and model confidence. This implication is limited
to short review tasks with an early prototype.

# Limitations

The sample, task duration, and prototype maturity limit transferability.
