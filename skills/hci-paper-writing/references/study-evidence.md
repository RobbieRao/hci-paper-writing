# HCI Study and Evidence Reporting

## Minimum Reporting Fields

| Field | Report |
|---|---|
| Participants or cases | Number, recruitment, inclusion, relevant characteristics, expertise, compensation |
| Context and tasks | Setting, scenario, realism, materials, duration, order, randomization or counterbalancing where relevant |
| System or materials | Prototype, interface, stimuli, dataset, baseline, and condition differences |
| Procedure | Consent through debrief, including deviations and failed sessions |
| Measures or data | Why each measure, trace, interview, observation, or artifact addresses an RQ |
| Analysis | Statistical model or qualitative analytic process, assumptions, uncertainty, researcher role |
| Ethics | Applicable review process, consent, privacy, identifiable media, risk, data-access limits |
| Boundaries | Population, culture, geography, task, setting, deployment duration, prototype maturity |

## Evidence Quality Checks

- Does each RQ have an explicit analysis path?
- Is the population or case selection appropriate for the claim?
- Are exclusions, missing data, negative cases, and deviations visible?
- Are qualitative themes grounded in enough situated evidence?
- Are quantitative results accompanied by uncertainty and practical meaning?
- Are mixed methods integrated around the same claims rather than juxtaposed?
- Does the paper distinguish observed behavior, participant report, researcher
  interpretation, and system performance?

## Construct Claims

Require explicit operationalization or careful wording for constructs such as
trust, understanding, transparency, interpretability, usefulness, agency,
collaboration, engagement, workload, fairness, safety, accessibility, and
adoption.

If a construct was not measured, use a narrower statement such as
`participants reported`, `the observed task behavior suggests`, or `the themes
indicate`.

## Human Evaluation of AI Systems

- Match evaluators to the claim: domain experts for correctness, target users for
  use experience, and appropriately scoped raters for perception judgments.
- Distinguish model performance from human experience.
- Report assignment, interfaces, instructions, items, raters, agreement or
  uncertainty, attention checks where justified, and compensation.
- Explain what `interpretation` means: mechanism, feature influence, user mental
  model, decision support, or actionable control.
