# CHI 2027 Review Calibration

Use for CHI 2027 author-side red-teaming, panel synthesis, and revision planning.
Policy snapshot checked on October 26, 2026. Recheck live sources for decisions
that depend on the current review form or process.

This reference uses public guidance and original coaching examples. It does not
contain confidential submissions, reviewer reports, committee discussion, or
empirical claims about private reviewing behavior.

## Official Criteria

The [review process](https://chi2027.acm.org/authors/papers/papers-review-process/)
identifies five dimensions:

| Dimension | Question for the author |
|---|---|
| Originality | What ideas or approaches does the paper introduce? |
| Correctness | Can readers assess the process and its rigor within this research tradition? |
| Novelty | What remains new after comparison with relevant prior work? |
| Importance | Who benefits, and why is that benefit significant? |
| Clarity of exposition | Can readers recover the argument and evidence? |

The external-review recommendation choices are A (minor revisions), ARR
(A or RR), RR (revise and resubmit), RRX (RR or reject), and X (reject). The
AC synthesizes reviews and discussion; these labels are not independent final
decisions. Round two ends in acceptance or rejection rather than another major
revision round.

## Match the Contribution

The [reviewing guide](https://chi2027.acm.org/guide-to-reviewing-papers/)
centers contribution to HCI. It describes roughly 650 words as typical for a
substantive review, not a mandatory quota. A focused short paper should receive
demands proportionate to its contribution. Constructive replication can be
valuable; a newly invented technique is not the only route to contribution.

The [submission guide](https://chi2027.acm.org/guide-to-a-successful-submission/)
connects research quality to the claimed benefit and contribution type. Its
transparency guidance differentiates reproducible technical or quantitative
work from qualitative accounts of interpretation, decisions, and positionality.
It also stresses that the revision window cannot accommodate an extensive new
implementation, evaluation, or complete rewrite.

## Turn Criteria into a Repair Plan

The following is this Skill's coaching procedure, not an official CHI rubric:

1. State the strongest defensible contribution before listing weaknesses.
2. For each consequential concern, identify the manuscript evidence, affected
   claim, and smallest repair that would restore that claim.
3. Classify the repair as existing-evidence clarification, reanalysis of existing
   material, new evidence collection, or contribution redesign.
4. Check dependencies and available time. Separate confidence that a repair is
   possible from evidence that it is already complete.
5. If the user requests a recommendation rehearsal, explain the decisive repair
   condition. An RRX label needs an explicit unresolved question whose answer
   would move the judgment toward RR or X. Do not map sentiment to a label.

Use this compact table when it makes a revision decision easier:

| Concern | Affected claim | Existing evidence | Minimum repair | Time/dependencies | Evidence that changes the judgment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not demand an additional study merely to make a paper resemble another
research tradition. Conversely, editing the discussion cannot supply an outcome
the study never investigated.

## Resolve Disagreement by Its Cause

In an authorized author-side feedback set, distinguish:

- **Contribution reading:** reviewers attribute different central claims to the
  same manuscript. Clarify the claim before trying to reconcile judgments.
- **Evidence reading:** reviewers inspected different artifacts or interpreted
  an analysis differently. Identify the exact unresolved evidence question.
- **Repairability:** reviewers agree on a weakness but disagree about whether
  the required work fits the revision window. Specify the repair and its cost.
- **Method standard:** reviewers apply different standards of rigor. Revisit the
  declared research tradition and the claim those standards are meant to test.

Count a concern once even when several readers repeat it. Preserve a supported
minority concern; repetition and reviewer expertise help interpret a disagreement
but do not settle factual questions. Explain the reason for a requested change
so the author can address its substance.

## Keep Process Statements Current

The general process page describes an AI-generated ADR rubric report. However,
the [dedicated tools announcement](https://chi2027.acm.org/2026/08/29/ai-assisted-tools-in-the-chi-2027-papers-review-process-what-they-do-what-they-do-not-do-and-how-humans-remain-responsible/)
explicitly says Tool 4 will not provide feedback in the CHI 2027 review process.
Record that discrepancy; do not tell authors that an official automated quality
rubric will assess their paper. The announcement separately describes submission
completeness, matching, and screening support. It keeps consequential decisions
with people and treats screening flags as evidence for inspection.

The Skill's panel is an author rehearsal. It has no committee authority, and its
output must not be represented as official CHI feedback.
