# Rebuttal and Revision

Use for `hci-rebuttal`, author responses, revise-and-resubmit letters,
shepherding, and camera-ready commitments. The response is not complete until
each promise is linked to a manuscript change or an explicit limitation.

## Normalize Before Drafting

Give each comment a stable ID such as `R2-C3`. Separate the reviewer's wording
from the author's interpretation, then classify the underlying concern:

- misunderstanding caused by manuscript visibility;
- valid weakness;
- missing or hidden existing evidence;
- request for new evidence;
- scope or overclaim;
- novelty or positioning;
- method-tradition mismatch;
- ethics, privacy, accessibility, or policy;
- local clarity or presentation.

Merge comments only when they have the same underlying concern. Preserve
reviewer-specific differences.

## Response Unit

For each material concern, write in this order:

1. direct answer;
2. evidence already available, with an exact paper or artifact location;
3. concession or scope boundary where warranted;
4. concrete manuscript change;
5. verification test.

Do not claim that new evidence exists when it does not. Do not promise a new
study, analysis, system feature, or policy-compliance change unless it is
feasible in the actual response or revision window.

## Closed Ledger

Use the workspace ledgers or return this table:

| ID | Concern | Response claim | Evidence | Manuscript action | Location | Verification | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Allowed statuses are `open`, `planned`, `drafted`, `done`, `blocked`, and
`accepted-boundary`. Mark `done` only after opening the revised location and
checking that the promised change exists and remains consistent elsewhere.

## Priority

1. Correct factual misunderstandings that could change the decision.
2. Address central contribution, validity, ethics, or evidence concerns.
3. Surface existing evidence that reviewers missed.
4. Narrow claims when the evidence cannot support their original scope.
5. Resolve repeated clarity and figure problems.
6. Handle local wording and optional suggestions last.

## Rebuttal Gate

Before returning a response, check:

- every major reviewer question has an ID and disposition;
- factual answers point to real evidence;
- concessions are explicit rather than hidden in hedging;
- every `we will` statement appears in the revision ledger;
- promised changes fit the available time and venue rules;
- the tone is direct, calm, and written for a committee reader;
- no confidential reviewer material is copied into an unauthorized service;
- current response limits and allowed content were verified from official venue
  sources.

After revision, rerun the deterministic audit and the reviewer lens that raised
the concern. A polished response without a verified manuscript change is still
an open item.
