# HCI Red-team Checklist

## Contribution Lens

- Can a reader state the primary contribution in one sentence?
- Does it advance HCI rather than only an adjacent technical field?
- Is novelty established against the right scholarly conversation?
- Do title, abstract, contribution list, and discussion make the same promise?
- Is the claimed significance proportional to the evidence?

## Method Lens

- Does the chosen method fit the research question and contribution tradition?
- Are actors, cases, context, materials, procedure, and analysis transparent?
- Does every major claim have traceable evidence?
- Are alternative explanations, negative cases, uncertainty, and boundaries
  treated honestly?
- Are ethics and researcher responsibilities visible where needed?

## Reader and Venue Lens

- Can the paper be reconstructed from title, abstract, teaser, contributions,
  and findings headings?
- Does the main text stand alone?
- Is the target track or community explicit?
- Are figures legible and claim-bearing rather than decorative?
- Are limitations survivable boundaries or evidence that the core claim fails?
- Would a reviewer have a memorable, defensible reason to champion the work?

## Risk Discipline

For every criticism, provide:

- manuscript location or quoted evidence;
- severity;
- affected claim;
- why the issue matters under the selected method lens;
- minimum credible repair;
- verification step.

Do not invent missing content, demand fashionable methods without claim-based
justification, or convert personal preference into a venue requirement.
