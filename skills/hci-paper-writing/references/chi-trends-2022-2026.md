# CHI Research Trends, 2022–2026

Use this atlas when a user asks what CHI has been publishing, which topics are
growing, how a topic is changing, or what may matter for CHI 2027. It is a
directional map for topic selection and positioning—not an acceptance model.

## Evidence Base

This snapshot groups indexed papers from the public CHI main proceedings for
2022–2026 into 50 analyst-created topic groups using paper-level titles and
abstracts. The groups are not ACM subcommittees or official topic labels. A
paper is assigned to one primary group, so interdisciplinary work is inevitably
simplified.

Official proceedings:

- [CHI 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3491102)
- [CHI 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548)
- [CHI 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904)
- [CHI 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598)
- [CHI 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318)

The indexed annual corpus grows substantially across these years. Interpret a
topic's share of the annual corpus alongside its raw paper count: a topic can
gain papers while losing attention relative to a faster-growing conference.
Counts below are `2022 / 2023 / 2024 / 2025 / 2026` within this snapshot, not
submission totals, acceptance rates, or an official ACM census.

## The Five-Year Shift

The clearest change is not simply “more AI papers.” CHI work is moving from
generic AI capability demonstrations toward situated questions about agency,
professional practice, governance, care, education, creativity, identity, and
failure. The most useful distinction is between a technology label and the HCI
problem it now carries.

|---|---:|---|---|

### Mature, persistent conversations

These areas remain substantial but do not show the same clean acceleration:

- explanation, trust, and ethical decisions in human–AI teams;
- haptic XR, material interaction, and wearable feedback;
- VR avatars, embodiment, and perspective-taking;
- accessible visualization for blind and low-vision users;
- games, emotion, empathy, and wellbeing;
- Soma Design, living materials, sound, and embodied art;
- family smart homes, privacy, and parent–child negotiation;
- ageing, caregivers, and enrichment technologies.

For a mature topic, novelty usually comes from a sharper mechanism, population,
context, longitudinal boundary, or research tradition—not from attaching a new
model to a familiar task.

## CHI 2027 Scenarios

These are evidence-informed scenarios, not predictions of paper counts or
acceptance. Use them to generate questions and counter-scenarios.

### High confidence

1. **From AI assistant to negotiated agency.** Papers will increasingly ask who
   may delegate what, how users contest outputs, and how systems repair failures.
2. **From prototype novelty to situated deployment.** Professional creative
   work, classrooms, healthcare, public services, and organizations will demand
   evidence about adoption, coordination, maintenance, and unintended effects.
3. **From accuracy to epistemic interaction.** Provenance, uncertainty,
   verification, calibrated reliance, and responsibility will matter more than
   reporting model performance alone.
4. **From individual use to multi-party consequences.** Bystanders, teachers,
   caregivers, coworkers, institutions, data workers, and affected non-users
   become part of the unit of analysis.

### Medium confidence

5. **Embodied and spatial generative interaction.** Generative media will move
   further into XR, physical materials, wearables, performance, and cultural
   expression—but contribution must come from embodied experience, not novelty
   spectacle.
6. **Longitudinal AI relationships.** Self-concept, emotional attachment,
   dependence, learning, and behavior change will push studies beyond one-session
   usability evaluations.
7. **Accessibility as method and governance.** More work will treat disabled
   people as co-producers of knowledge and examine who controls sensing,
   adaptation, and representation.
8. **AI-mediated research practice.** Qualitative analysis, literature work, and
   study operations are likely to draw attention, but the current historical
   signal is unstable; transparency and epistemic validity will be the real HCI
   contribution.

### Wildcards

- sustainability costs and material consequences of large-scale AI;
- synthetic identity, likeness, and consent across generated media;
- regulation-driven interface changes and organizational compliance work;
- collective refusal, non-use, and deliberately non-AI alternatives.

## How to Use the Atlas

When coaching a topic or manuscript:

1. identify the closest topic group and its five-year trajectory;
2. state whether the manuscript follows, reframes, or challenges that trajectory;
3. name the crowded default contribution it must avoid;
4. identify one adjacent trend that creates a method-transfer opportunity;
5. inspect current papers before making any closest-work or novelty claim;
6. present 2027 statements as scenarios with confidence and disconfirming signs.

## Limits

- This is an analysis of accepted proceedings, not the unseen submission pool.
- A larger topic is not a better topic, and a growing topic is not easier to
  publish in.
- Topic labels compress interdisciplinary work and can shift when the corpus or
  grouping changes.
- Overall proceedings growth makes raw counts misleading without annual shares.
- Historical backtesting preserves broad topic prevalence better than it ranks
  future growth. Precise “top topic for 2027” claims are not supported.
- Verify newer official proceedings and policies when using this atlas after its
  stated coverage period.
