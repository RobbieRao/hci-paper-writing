# HCI Section Patterns

## Related Work

Organize by research conversation, not by paper. For each conversation:

1. define the line of work;
2. synthesize what it enables or explains;
3. identify a scope, assumption, tension, or open question with support;
4. position the present contribution without manufacturing a gap.

Treat `however`, `little is known`, `limited`, and `fails to` as strong claims
that need citations or analysis.

## Introduction

Use four moves:

1. establish the human, interaction, design, or sociotechnical stakes;
2. synthesize what existing work leaves unresolved;
3. explain the paper's approach and evidence path;
4. state contributions that later sections demonstrably support.

## Method, System, or Design Process

Make the main paper sufficient to judge research quality. Clarify target actors,
context, tasks, rationale, procedure, materials or implementation boundaries,
analysis, researcher role where relevant, ethics, and limitations.

## Findings or Results

Write around claims rather than chronology:

1. claim-first heading or topic sentence;
2. representative evidence;
3. warranted interpretation;
4. boundary or counterexample.

## Discussion

Explain what the findings change for design, research, theory, practice, policy,
or ethics. Derive implications from findings and name their scope. Do not repeat
results or present generic design recommendations.

## Figures

Prefer figures that make interaction and evidence visible:

- problem scenario;
- user action and system feedback loop;
- interface or system overview;
- study procedure;
- RQ-method-finding-implication map;
- qualitative mechanism or conceptual framework.

Make captions self-contained and state the claim each figure supports.
