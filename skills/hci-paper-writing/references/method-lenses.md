# Method Lenses

Select the lens that matches how the paper can legitimately know what it claims.

## Qualitative Inquiry

Check sampling logic, context, researcher positionality where relevant, data
generation, analytic procedure, reflexivity, negative or deviant cases,
evidentiary excerpts, and transferability. Do not reduce rigor to sample size or
inter-rater agreement when those are inappropriate to the method.

## Quantitative or Experimental Work

Check construct validity, power or precision rationale, conditions, assignment,
confounds, exclusions, model assumptions, uncertainty, effect magnitude,
multiplicity where relevant, and practical HCI meaning. Statistical significance
alone does not establish contribution significance.

## Design Research and Research-through-Design

Check the framing of the design situation, process transparency, material or
experiential specificity, intermediate knowledge, reflection, situatedness,
alternatives considered, and how the artifact produces knowledge. Do not demand
a controlled experiment unless the claims require causal comparison.

## Systems and Interaction Techniques

Check the new interaction capability, task and user context, system boundary,
implementation detail proportional to the claim, comparison choice, feasibility,
latency or reliability when interaction depends on it, and ecological use value.

## Field, Deployment, and CSCW Work

Check institutional and social context, stakeholder relationships, duration,
infrastructure, appropriation, breakdowns, power, coordination, and temporal
change. Avoid treating deployment logs as self-explanatory evidence.

## Mixed Methods

Check why methods are combined, where integration occurs, how disagreements are
handled, and whether the combined evidence supports a claim unavailable to either
method alone.
