# Synthetic Review Calibration Exercises

These original fictional cases illustrate the public CHI guidance. They are not
anonymized versions of real submissions. Use the prompts before consulting the
inspection notes below. A rehearsal result is not an official recommendation.

## Case A: A Short Replication

An author submits a focused short paper that repeats a published menu-selection
study on a low-cost device. It reports an informative null result, documents the
protocol and analysis, and limits its claim to a boundary condition. One reader
says it lacks a newly invented technique. Another asks for a year-long deployment.

Task: Diagnose contribution fit and propose revisions proportionate to the
paper's scope. Identify what additional evidence, if any, the claim requires.

## Case B: Two Repairs with Different Costs

A transit-display prototype claims to help riders recover from service changes.
Its existing task study measures recovery success and time. The appendix holds
the full task definitions, but the main paper leaves the outcomes hard to
interpret. The same paper also claims to improve independent travel across a
city, although it contains no field evidence about that outcome.

Task: Separate what can be repaired by surfacing existing evidence from what
requires narrowing the claim or collecting new evidence. Give the author a
feasible revision plan, without inventing a field study or its results.

## Case C: Different Readings of a Design Contribution

A fictional tactile calendar study presents design knowledge about how a small
community negotiates shared schedules. A reader evaluates it as a universal
productivity intervention; another reads it as a situated account of material
practice. Both want the contribution statement clarified, but for different
reasons. No final venue recommendation has been supplied.

Task: Explain the disagreement, state the evidence each interpretation would
need, and identify the smallest honest contribution the existing work supports.

## Inspection Notes

- Case A: Treat the replication's boundary knowledge as a possible contribution.
  The reader's preference for invention or long deployment is not sufficient
  reason to require either. Verify whether the replication and null-result
  interpretation actually support the stated boundary.
- Case B: Existing task definitions can repair visibility. They cannot establish
  independent city travel. A useful response must distinguish those two claims
  and track the promised manuscript changes.
- Case C: Resolve the contribution reading first. Neither a majority vote nor an
  average sentiment supplies the missing claim-to-evidence match. Do not invent
  a committee outcome.
